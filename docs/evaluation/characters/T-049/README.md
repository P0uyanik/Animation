# T-049: hair approach study (strand groom vs sculpted vs hybrid)

Date: 2026-11-17. Blender: bpy module 5.3.0 (Linux, CPU, Cycles + OIDN), driven through `tools/bpy_blender.py`.
Status: **study / experimental.** Nothing is published, approved or promoted. No shared generator, spec, tool or
engine file was changed; prototype code is in `pipeline/charsys/experiments/hair_study/`.

## Recommendation

**Use approach C, the hybrid, for hair v3 (T-044):** felted base volumes plus a limited number of broad felt clumps or
locks at the edges, the fringe and the buns. This judgement is visual, against the owner's references. It is the only
approach that kept both characters' faces clear *and* read as felted plush hair rather than ropes or a helmet. The
owner's own hair blueprint
([21_boy_hair_geometry_groom_material_spec.webp](../../../references/boy_pipeline/21_boy_hair_geometry_groom_material_spec.webp),
"Blender implementation: HYBRID METHOD") describes the same construction.

The recommendation comes with these conditions; T-044 should treat them as part of the approach:

1. **The face keep-out is spec data.** Every lock or clump tip with |azimuth| below the keep-out angle stays above the
   brow elevation. This is the single largest fix for "covers the face". It worked for all three approaches, but only A
   still needed extra rules for an under-layer.
2. **Edge locks stay few and broad:** about 50–60 clumps for a short spiky style, and 10–15 ringlets plus a sculpted
   base for long curly hair. Many narrow locks bring the rope read straight back (approach A).
3. **No voxel fusion in production.** Fusing (B) reads as one helmet, removes all secondary motion and costs 3–6× the
   vertices.
4. **The girl's back hair needs more distinct ringlets on top of a thinner sculpted base.** At 7 back locks the C
   curtain still reads as a regular ribbed surface. The reference shows chunky separate ringlets.

## What was built

The same hairstyle layout, read from the references, is built three ways for each character. All three share the
same flow, the same felt material preset (`felt hair`) and the same shared GN fibre layers.

| | Boy (spiky felt clumps, whorl at the back of the crown) | Girl (high bun, felted crown, side-swept fringe, long ringlets) |
|---|---|---|
| **A** strand/lock groom | scalp cap + 223 narrow felt locks (each C clump split in 2–3, plus a 70-lock under-layer); 181 spring chains | cap + 80 yarn locks swept to a yarn bun + 7 yarn fringe locks + 42 felt ringlet locks; 43 chains |
| **B** sculpted shells | cap + felted base volume + C's 55 clump shapes, fused by voxel remesh (2 mm) into one smoothed sculpt; no chains | felted crown/fringe/temple/bun shells + a carved ringlet curtain, fused into one sculpt; no chains |
| **C** hybrid | cap + felted base volume (`felt_volume` gather) + 55 broad felt clumps (6 crown tufts, 14 top, 6 fringe, 10 temples, 19 back); 41 chains | cap + felted crown/fringe/temple/bun shells + a sculpted ringlet curtain + 11 felt ringlets at the edges; 12 chains |

Code:
- `primitives.py` adds two experiment primitives, registered into the shared `groom/1` builder at runtime, so the
  shared file is never edited:
  - `study_clumps`: leaf-shaped, flattened, pointed felt clumps that follow the scalp along a flow map (away from the
    whorl, down, or standing) and include a face keep-out;
  - `study_curtain`: a sculpted long-hair shell with ringlet columns, helical lobes and scalloped tips.
  
  It also adds `fuse()`, the voxel-remesh union used by B. Clumps record dynamics chains exactly like groom locks.
- `study_build.py` opens an unchanged master from `build_character.py`, removes its hair and chain bones, and builds the
  study hair with `groom.build`/`groom.bind` (library calls). It then adds fibres and binds to the rig.
- The specs are generated by `specs/make_boy_specs.py` and `specs/make_girl_specs.py`. The girl's specs are derived
  from her current spec, which is unchanged.
- `render_evidence.sh` produces the evidence stills; `measure.py` produces the geometric measurements and the head
  turn; `make_sheets.py` and `scorecard.py` produce the sheets and the scorecard. The README in the experiment folder
  has the commands.

Notes on the numbers:
- **Fidelity is not a ranking by itself.**
  - For the girl, B has the best hair IoU (its fused curtain is wider and fills the silhouette), yet visually it is
    the weakest: a single helmet with carved corn-rows.
  - For the boy, the committed baseline hair (`current`, T-032: the legacy hair, tuned on exactly these views) scores
    hair IoU 0.785 against 0.67 for C. The study layout is ~20% smaller in area than the reference (area ratio
    ~0.8, `hair_area` −23%), so it is a layout limitation, not a property of the approach.
  - Overall fidelity scores (boy 55–56 vs 64.5 baseline) are **not comparable** to the baseline. That baseline also
    includes the 1.1.0 eyelid override and was rendered at 32 samples.
- Face visibility is measured on a 41×41 grid of rays over the face window (brow top to mouth, eye corners ±45 mm).
  Below 1.0 on the boy in all three approaches is intended: the reference fringe reaches the brows and the temple
  clumps reach eye level. The eyes themselves stay ≥ 97% visible.
- Collision counts sampled hair vertices more than 2 mm inside the ears, neck, body or clothing (head and scalp
  excluded, because roots and base insets sit there by design). In the turn, girl A's long ringlets swing into the
  shoulders and arms (0.06% → 0.43%). The head-turn frames show the same for C's two front edge ringlets (a brown
  patch on the upper arm in frames 6–9). The head-turn proxy has **no collision response**; the engine solver has
  colliders (`hair.dynamics.colliders`), and that was not run here.
- Spec size is similar for all three (7–11 kB) because they share one layout. The reuse marks judge how *easy* a new
  hairstyle is to author, not how long the file is.

## Limitations and deviations (honest list)

- **Render budget:** 24 evidence stills (6 approaches × 3 fidelity views + 1 close-up). The rules are not fully met:
  - **3 extra evidence-quality stills** were rendered and discarded: a first boy C fidelity run before retuning. That
    is 27 in total, **3 over the budget of 24**.
  - The fidelity tool also renders flat 1-sample region-id images for its measurements (18).
  - Iteration previews were 15 strips × 4 tiny head crops (300 px, 4 samples, no fibres). Workbench/EEVEE need a GPU
    that this container lacks, so these were Cycles renders.
  - Head turns: 6 × 24 frames. About 40 more girl-B frames were rendered by two runs stopped by the 10-minute command
    cap (frames overwritten, identical settings).
- **Close-ups crop the top of the hair.** The close-up camera was placed too low. It still shows the face and fringe,
  which is what it is for. I did not re-render, to stay near the budget. The boy A/B head-turn crops also cut the
  crown (rendered before the camera was reframed); C and all girl strips use the wider framing.
- **The head turn is a proxy, not the engine.** It is a damped torsional spring per chain about the vertical axis,
  with the spec's stiffness and damping and the lag spread over the chain's bones, driven on the rig's `head` control
  (yaw 40°·sin(πt), peak 126°/s). There is no gravity, no collision response and no bun swing beyond yaw. The engine's
  secondary-motion layer (TD-38 package `dynamics`) should be checked in T-044 on the chosen approach.
- **Colour:** the girl's hair reads redder than the reference in all three approaches. Her material assignment was
  reused unchanged (`felt hair`, base colour 86/54/44), so this is a material/lighting matter, not an approach matter.
  The boy uses `felt hair` at 44/38/36. He has no hair assignment in his spec; this value comes from his target
  colour 48/39/35.
- **Boy base:** built from his current spec without the unpublished 1.1.0 eyelid override (irrelevant for hair). His
  skin looks darker and more orange under character_neutral/1 lighting in every approach; this was not investigated
  (out of scope).
- **The boy's crown tufts** are smaller and more uniform than the reference's large irregular top tufts, and the whole
  layout is ~20% under the reference area. T-044 should size the volume from the reference silhouette.
- **Experiment-only runtime patching:** `primitives.register()` adds two builder types and makes
  `groom.validate_dynamics` accept `study_clumps` as locks, **in the study process only**. A production version needs
  these as real shared primitives (T-044).
- Not tested: the engine export/import of the study hair, determinism of the voxel fusion, GPU render times, and any
  third hairstyle beyond the two characters. A synthetic third style was not built.
